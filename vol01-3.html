How a Three-Vote Split Cost the NNP the Capital

The NDC didn't ride a wave into the Town of St. George. The opposition divided its own vote, and the NNP's wounds were self-inflicted, opened nearly two years before polling day. Claudette Joseph won with 1,269 votes. Peter David and Troy Noel had 1,266 between them.

What can be done: a four-year plan for the capital

The Town of St. George does not need a political earthquake. It needs the NNP to repair what it broke.

Year one, 2027: heal the split. Open talks with Mr. David's supporters and with the branch members who resigned, and create a clear route back for any who wish to return. Rebuild a working constituency executive, with officers who live in the seat.

Year two, 2028: choose a candidate who lives in the constituency, through the party's own process and endorsed by all its factions, and commit to that person for two elections. Open a permanent office in town.

Year three, 2029: give each of the 12 polling divisions a captain. Hold the National Stadium divisions, where a united opposition already leads, and win back the older town divisions at Marryshow House and the SDA School, where the margin was a handful of votes.

Year four, 2030: run on a few local commitments, repeated everywhere, rather than a long manifesto. Serve year-round. Help that arrives only at campaign time reads as transactional; show up when nothing is at stake.

The lesson for every NNP seat

The Town of St. George was not lost to the NDC. It was lost to a division that began inside the NNP, at a convention nearly two years before polling day. Every other volume in this series will return to the same theme: in 2026, the NNP's own decisions decided how badly each seat went.

In the capital, unity alone would have been worth the seat.

What it means for 2030/31

  1. Heal the split first: unity alone was worth the seat in 2026.
  2. Own the branch: keep a working constituency executive years before the writ.
  3. Live in the seat, and be chosen early through the party's own process.
  4. Win on your own brand, strong enough to hold against the national tide.
  5. Three promises, not hundreds: a few local commitments, repeated everywhere.
  6. Serve year-round: help that arrives only at campaign time reads as transactional.
  7. Hold the National Stadium divisions, where a united opposition already leads.
  8. Win back the old-town divisions decided by a handful of votes.
