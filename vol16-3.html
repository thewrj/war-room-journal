The Verdict: How the NNP Lost Grenada, How the NDC Won It, and the Road Back for 2030/31

Fifteen constituencies, 282 polling divisions and one conclusion: the NNP did not lose 14 seats on polling day, but over four years, through decisions of its own making, while the NDC executed a plan. The way back is narrower than it looks, and shorter than it feels: 1,765 voters in seven seats.

A fair verdict must also credit the winner. The NDC did not win 14 seats by accident, and an opposition that does not understand how it was beaten will be beaten again.

It chose the battlefield and the date

Prime Minister Dickon Mitchell advised dissolution on 2nd October 2026, with nominations set for 15th October and polling for 5th November, almost a year before the constitutional deadline. A governing party with a record to run on, facing an opposition still organising, is best served by a short campaign, and that is precisely what the NDC chose.

It kept its candidates and worked its seats

Every NDC member elected in 2022 who stood again in 2026 increased their majority: Andy Williams from 1,068 to 2,792 in St. George South; Ron Redhead from 1,087 to 2,027 in St. George North East; Phillip Telesford from 488 to 1,294 in St. George South East; Kerryne James from 7 to 573 in St. John; Lennox Andrews from 425 to 1,094 in St. Andrew South West; Joseph Andall from 268 to 1,160 in St. Patrick West; Tevin Andrews from 149 to 976 in Carriacou; and the Prime Minister himself from 1,672 to 3,180 in St. David.

In most of those seats the NDC's share of the whole electorate held steady or rose. Its members were present between elections, and the voters rewarded them for it.

It gave its people visible roles

The NDC used government to raise the profile of its members in their own seats. Mr. Williams served repeatedly as Acting Prime Minister; Mr. Andall became Foreign Minister; Ms. James held the climate and environment portfolio; and Mr. Andrew, after losing in 2022, became Minister of Education and returned to win. Each appointment made the local member a national figure in the eyes of their own constituents.

It welcomed defectors and targeted NNP seats

When Ms. Thomas left the NNP in 2023, the NDC brought her into government, and she held St. Andrew North West in its colours. The party then pursued the NNP's remaining seats, with the Prime Minister singling out St. Mark when he announced the election. Of the six seats the NNP held in 2022, the NDC took five: St. Andrew South East, St. Andrew North East, St. Andrew North West, St. Mark and the Town of St. George.

It governed through a disaster and was credited for it

Hurricane Beryl devastated Carriacou and Petite Martinique in July 2024. The government controlled the recovery, and the NDC's member there, the party's chairman, was its local face; his majority rose from 149 to 976. Incumbency in a crisis is a powerful advantage, and the NDC used it.

None of this constitutes a secret method. It is what disciplined parties do: select candidates early, keep them in place, give them visible work, and turn up between elections. The NNP did all of these things in its years of dominance, and it can do them again.
