The Revolving Door: How the NNP Gave Away Grenada's Biggest Seat

St. George South holds the airport, Grand Anse, the university and most of the hotels. The NNP has sent it four candidates in four elections, lost it by 2,792 votes, and failed to win a single polling division. Here is how it could start again.

By Greg HostenJan. 7, 2027 · 8 min read

What can be done: a four-year plan for the south

St. George South will not be won back by the next caretaker named eighteen months before an election. It can be won only by a party that starts now and treats the seat as a four-year campaign. The steps are not complicated. They have simply never been tried here.

Year one, 2027: choose a candidate who lives in the constituency, and commit publicly to keeping that person for at least two elections, win or lose. Open a permanent office on the Grand Anse corridor with paid staff, funded centrally by the party rather than left to the caretaker. Map all 33 polling divisions, from Springs to Calliste, and build a household list of the 5,510 who stayed home and the voters who have drifted away since 2018.

Year two, 2028: run a listening programme by sector, not just by village: hotel and restaurant workers, airport staff, SGU employees and students' landlords, small businesses and vendors on the beach strip. Publish what residents say their top problems are, in their words, and build a short local programme around three of them. Register new and young voters every month, not every election.

Year three, 2029: hold the sitting member to account in public with a quarterly constituency report card, measured against the government's own promises for the south. Recruit a captain for each of the 33 polling divisions and train volunteers to contact every household on the list at least three times a year. Start with the eight Blessed Sacrament divisions and Springs, where the NNP's vote held up best.

Year four, 2030: campaign on a record of presence. By then the NNP candidate should have attended more community events, solved more individual problems and been seen in more neighbourhoods than any challenger in the seat's history. Set honest targets: win back the 1,116 voters lost since 2022, then reach into the non-voters. Recovering the 2022 vote alone would cut the margin by more than a third. Winning the seat means persuading roughly 1,400 people to switch, or finding nearly 2,800 new supporters.

The lesson for every NNP seat

Mr. Williams has shown the method. He was local, he was visible, and he turned office into presence. In 2022 he won by bringing new voters to the polls. In 2026 he held them while the NNP's own supporters stayed home.

The NNP's failure here is not that it lost to him. It is that it never seriously tried to become the party of the south. Until it does, St. George South will remain what the last four elections have made it: a revolving door for the NNP, and a home for whoever the NDC sends there.

What it means for 2030/31

  1. Pick a local candidate early, and keep them for two elections.
  2. Fund the caretaker centrally: an office and staff are the minimum.
  3. Do not parachute in: the 2022 candidate lost the south, then lost Carriacou.
  4. Chase the 5,510 non-voters, not just the party faithful.
  5. Organise by sector: hotels, airport, SGU and small business.
  6. Recruit a captain for each of the 33 polling divisions.
  7. Publish a quarterly report card on the sitting MP.
  8. Do not mistake a minister's personal vote for a party base.
