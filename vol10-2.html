The Revolving Door: How the NNP Gave Away Grenada's Biggest Seat

St. George South holds the airport, Grand Anse, the university and most of the hotels. The NNP has sent it four candidates in four elections, lost it by 2,792 votes, and failed to win a single polling division. This volume sets out how it might begin again.

WhatsAppXFacebook

The polling-division returns are the most unforgiving evidence of all. The NNP did not carry a single one of the seat's 33 divisions. Its best result anywhere was 37.8 per cent of the two-party vote, at Blessed Sacrament R.C. School; its worst was 20.6 per cent, at the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints polling station; and in the two Woburn Community Centre divisions it took barely one vote in four.

There is no NNP stronghold in the south from which to build, and no village where the party can claim its message is working. Every rebuilding plan must start from the same place: a constituency where, at best, three voters in eight chose the NNP.

A caretaker without a base

The NNP named Christopher DeAllie Jr. caretaker on 23rd April 2025, about 19 months before the vote. He is a business and finance professional with a degree in business management and a postgraduate diploma in financial planning, and he founded what the party described as the island's first indoor mushroom farm. On paper, that is a credible profile for a seat full of entrepreneurs and professionals.

Nineteen months, however, is not long in a constituency of 12,000 voters, and the support around him was thin. In 2025 The New Today reported speculation that several new NNP caretakers, Mr. DeAllie among them, were working without party offices. In the same report a former NNP minister accused Dr. Keith Mitchell of not doing enough to help the new caretakers financially, adding that only the sitting NNP MPs had constituency offices, funded by the state. Against a sitting minister with a permanent office in Grand Anse, the NNP sent a newcomer without one.

In February 2026 the NNP announced that it was the first party to name a full slate of 15 candidates. A full slate is a press release; an office, a staff, a canvass list and a budget are a campaign. In the south, the NNP had the former without the latter.

Andy Williams made himself the seat

Mr. Williams, meanwhile, turned a single term into a fixture. He became Minister for Mobilisation, Implementation and Transformation, the NDC's General Secretary, and one of the ministers most often appointed Acting Prime Minister when Dickon Mitchell travelled, including in May 2024, over Christmas 2024 and in May 2026. Each appointment placed the MP for St. George South at the head of government in the eyes of his own voters.

His 4,637 votes were 608 more than in 2022, in a year when fewer people voted, and he won every polling division, from Springs to Calliste. That is what a constituency machine looks like.

The 5,510 who stayed home

The most damning number in St. George South is not a candidate's total. Turnout here this year was 54.5 per cent, the lowest of all 15 constituencies in Grenada: of 12,103 registered voters, 5,510 did not vote, more people than the entire electorate of the Town of St. George or of St. George North West. The register has grown by 1,200 names since 2022, yet 426 fewer ballots were cast.

The arithmetic of a comeback begins there. To overturn the 2026 result, the NNP would need 1,397 voters to switch sides, or 2,793 additional supporters to turn out, just over half of everyone who stayed home. That is a four-year undertaking, not a campaign. The airport workers, hotel staff, university employees and young families of the south are precisely the voters the NNP says it wants, and it has built nothing permanent with which to find them.
