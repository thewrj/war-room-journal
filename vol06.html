From Seven Votes to 573: How Kerryne James Locked Down St. John

In 2022 St. John was the closest seat in Grenada, won by the NDC by seven votes. Four years later the same two candidates met again and the margin was 573. The NNP's share fell less than the national swing, and it still lost ground in almost every corner of the parish.

By Greg HostenDec. 10, 2026 · 5 min read
WhatsAppXFacebook

No constituency in Grenada was closer in 2022 than St. John. The NDC's Kerryne James beat the NNP's Alvin DaBreo by 2,216 votes to 2,209, a margin of seven. On a night when the NDC won government by nine seats to six, St. John was the one that could have gone either way.

"A narrow defeat is an asset only if it is used. Seven votes became 573."Greg Hosten

On 5 November the same two candidates met again. Ms. James took 2,540 votes to Mr. DaBreo's 1,967. The DPM's Arley Gill took 82 and the GULP's Geoffrey Preudhomme 24. Seven votes became 573, and the most marginal seat in the country became a comfortable NDC hold.

The closest seat in the country

St. John covers Gouyave, the parish's fishing town, along with Grand Roy, Concord, Florida, Mount Granby and Clozier. It has long been competitive. Mr. DaBreo, a former NNP member for the seat, lost it in 2022 by the smallest margin in the election.

A seven-vote defeat is the best possible starting point for an opposition party. It means the seat is genuinely winnable, that the candidate is known, and that a few extra hours of canvassing in one village could change the result. The question for the NNP was what it would do with four years.

Four years on, the same contest, a different result

The answer is in the numbers. Between 2022 and 2026 the NNP's vote fell by 242, from 2,209 to 1,967. Ms. James's vote rose by 324, from 2,216 to 2,540. The NNP's share of the two-party vote fell from 49.9 per cent to 43.6 per cent.

That fall of 6.3 points is smaller than the national swing of 10.3 points. Mr. DaBreo ran ahead of his party's decline, which says something about his standing in the parish. But a seat lost by seven needed the NNP to go forward, not to fall back more slowly than elsewhere.

An incumbent who used her term

Ms. James became one of the most visible members of the NDC government, serving as Minister for Climate Resilience, the Environment and Renewable Energy. She entered the 2026 campaign as a sitting minister with four years of constituency work behind her, defending a seat she had won by the narrowest margin in the country.

A seven-vote majority is a warning to the winner as much as an opportunity for the loser. Ms. James appears to have treated it as a warning. Her vote grew by 324 in an election where total turnout in St. John fell.

The split was not the reason

St. John had four candidates in 2026. The DPM's Mr. Gill, an attorney, former CARICOM ambassador and the DPM's deputy leader, promised voters 'powerful' leadership and took 82 votes. The GULP took 24.

Even if every one of those 106 votes had gone to the NNP, Mr. DaBreo would still have lost by 467. Unlike the Town of St. George, where the DPM's vote decided the seat, the third parties in St. John were a footnote. The NNP's problem here was its own vote, not a divided opposition.

Gouyave went one way

The polling-division results show where the seat was decided. The NNP carried only 4 of the 21 divisions: Clozier, the Florida Medical Station and two of the three Concord divisions. Its best results were around 59 per cent of the two-party vote in each.

The NDC won Gouyave and its surroundings clearly. At the St. John's Anglican School the NNP took 38.4 per cent across two divisions, and in one division only 31.5 per cent. At the three St. John's R.C. School divisions it took 40.1 per cent. At Mount Granby it took 34.9 per cent. At Grand Roy, across four divisions, 40.8 per cent.

Florida was split. The NNP won the Florida Medical Station by 107 votes to 75 but lost all three divisions at the Florida Government School, by 381 votes to 298 between them. That pattern, holding a small station and losing the bigger school next door, repeats across the parish.

The voters who stayed home

St. John's register grew by 824 names between 2022 and 2026, but only 172 more ballots were cast. Turnout fell from 69.8 per cent to 64.2 per cent, and 2,580 registered voters did not vote, up from 1,928.

Measured against the whole electorate, the NNP won the votes of 34.6 per cent of all registered voters in 2022 and 27.3 per cent in 2026. Ms. James's share barely moved, from 34.7 to 35.2 per cent. The gap that decided the seat opened on the NNP side, among people who had voted for the party before and did not do so again.

Two hundred and eighty-seven voters

To have won the seat, the NNP needed 287 voters to switch sides, or 574 more of its own supporters to turn out. That second figure is about one in five of those who stayed home, and only a little more than double the 242 votes the party lost since 2022.

St. John is not lost. It is a seat the NNP had in its hands four years ago and allowed to drift. Restoring the 2022 vote alone would cut the deficit by more than 40 per cent.

What can be done: a four-year plan for St. John

St. John needs the NNP to treat it as what it was in 2022: the most winnable seat in the country.

Year one, 2027: settle the candidate early. If Mr. DaBreo stands again, give him a funded office in Gouyave and a full-time organiser. If not, name a successor in public in 2027, not in 2030. Rebuild a household list from the 2022 NNP vote, which was within seven votes of victory.

Year two, 2028: make Gouyave the centre of the campaign. The town's fishing community, vendors, young people and the workers at the nutmeg station need to see the NNP every month, not every five years. Hold regular meetings and build a short local programme from what people say. Register new voters monthly: the register grew by 824.

Year three, 2029: recruit a captain for each of the 21 polling divisions, with turnout targets based on the 2022 vote. Protect Clozier, Concord and the Florida Medical Station, and narrow the gap at the Florida Government School, Grand Roy and Mount Granby.

Year four, 2030: run the campaign as a turnout operation first. The target is 287 switchers or 574 new supporters, and the first step is simply to bring back the 242 NNP voters who did not vote for the party this time.

The lesson for every NNP seat

A narrow defeat is an asset only if it is used. In 2022 St. John was lost by seven votes. In 2026 it was lost by 573. The candidate did not change. What changed was four years in which the sitting member worked her seat and the NNP did not keep pace.

Every NNP seat lost narrowly in 2026, from St. Andrew South East to St. Mark, faces the same choice now. The four years after a close defeat decide the next result, not the four weeks before polling day.

What it means for 2030/31

  1. Use a narrow defeat: seven votes was the best start of any NNP seat.
  2. Settle the candidate early and fund an office in Gouyave.
  3. Win Gouyave: the town's divisions decided the seat.
  4. A smaller-than-national swing is not enough in a seat lost by seven.
  5. Do not blame the split: the DPM and GULP took only 106 votes.
  6. Bring back the 242 NNP voters who did not vote for the party in 2026.
  7. Captain all 21 divisions, with targets from the 2022 vote.
  8. Target 287 switchers or 574 new supporters.
