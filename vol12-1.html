Lennox Andrews Digs In: How the NNP Lost Ground in St. Andrew South West

The NNP won St. Andrew South West in 2013 and 2018 and lost it narrowly in 2022. In 2026 the NDC's majority more than doubled to 1,094. The NNP carried one polling division out of 20, and turnout fell nine points.

WhatsAppXFacebook

St. Andrew South West has changed hands with the national tide. The NDC's Sylvester Quarless won it in 2008. The NNP's Yolande Bain-Horsford took it in 2013 and held it in 2018. In 2022 the NDC's Lennox Andrews won it back, by 2,129 votes to 1,704.

"The NDC did not need to win over NNP voters. It needed them to stay home, and many did."Greg Hosten

On 5 November Mr. Andrews was re-elected by 2,428 votes to 1,334 for the NNP's Amanda Japal, in a straight two-way contest. A majority of 425 became 1,094. A seat the NNP held for nine years now looks like settled NDC ground.

A new caretaker, 19 months out

The NNP named Ms. Japal as its caretaker for St. Andrew South West in April 2025, one of four caretakers presented that month for seats the party lost in 2022. She had about 19 months before polling day to become known across a constituency of almost 6,000 voters, running from the edge of Grenville through Munich, Grand Bras, St. James and Harford Village to Birchgrove and Mount Carmel.

Against her was a sitting member with four years in the seat. The NNP's share of the two-party vote fell from 44.5 per cent to 35.5 per cent, a drop of 9.0 points, slightly less than the national swing of 10.3.

The NNP lost voters; the NDC kept its own

The NNP's vote fell by 370, from 1,704 to 1,334. The NDC's rose by 299. Measured against the whole electorate, Mr. Andrews won the votes of 40.0 per cent of all registered voters in 2022 and 40.7 per cent in 2026, almost unchanged. The NNP's share fell from 32.0 per cent to 22.4 per cent.

That pattern repeats across this series. Sitting NDC members held their share of the electorate steady, while the NNP's support drained away. The NDC did not need to win over NNP voters. It needed them to stay home, and many did.

Fewer voters than in 2022

St. Andrew South West's register grew by 635 names, but 80 fewer ballots were cast. Turnout fell from 72.4 per cent to 63.3 per cent, a drop of 9.0 points, the third-largest in the country. This year 2,187 registered voters did not vote, up from 1,472.

With no third party on the ballot, every one of those missing votes was a choice not to vote at all. In a seat the NNP held as recently as 2018, a fall in turnout of this size points to a party that did not reach its own supporters.
