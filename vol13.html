From 121 Votes: Two New Faces in St. Patrick East

St. Patrick East was the NDC's narrowest win in 2022 after St. John, decided by 121 votes. In 2026 both parties changed candidates. The NDC's newcomer grew the party's vote; the NNP's fell by 280, and the margin rose to 687.

By Greg HostenJan. 28, 2027 · 3 min read
WhatsAppXFacebook

St. Patrick East, covering River Sallee, Hermitage, Mount Rose, Morne Fendue and St. Ann's, was one of the closest contests in 2022. The NDC's Dennis Matthew-Cornwall beat the NNP's Pamela Moses by 1,443 votes to 1,322, a margin of 121.

"A new candidate with no time to become known is exposed to the full national swing."Greg Hosten

In 2026 neither of them was on the ballot. The NDC ran Carl Andall, and the NNP ran Robert Whyte. Mr. Andall won by 1,729 votes to 1,042, with 68 for the DPM's Dishon Edwards. The margin rose to 687.

A fair test of new candidates

Seats where both main parties change candidates are rare, and useful. Neither side had an incumbent's personal vote or a familiar name to rely on. The result measures the parties, their organisation and the national mood more cleanly than almost anywhere else.

By that measure, the NDC's organisation won comfortably. Its new candidate added 286 votes to the 2022 total. The NNP's new candidate lost 280. The NNP's share of the two-party vote fell from 47.8 per cent to 37.6 per cent, a drop of 10.2 points, almost exactly the national swing.

The national swing, with nothing to stop it

That near-perfect match with the national figure is the key finding. In seats where the NNP had a known candidate, such as St. John and St. Andrew North East, its fall was smaller than the national swing. In seats where the NDC had a long-serving incumbent, such as St. George South, the NNP's fall was larger.

In St. Patrick East there was no such local factor, and the NNP simply took the national hit. A 121-vote seat cannot absorb a ten-point swing. Without a local advantage of its own, the NNP lost a winnable seat by a clear margin.

Where the votes were

The NNP carried 2 of the 14 polling divisions: one at the River Sallee Government School, by 103 votes to 96, and one at St. Ann's Pre-Primary School, by 113 to 110. River Sallee was its best area overall, with 46.3 per cent of the two-party vote across four divisions. St. Ann's gave it 43.3 per cent.

Elsewhere the NDC won clearly. At the Mount Rose SDA School the NNP took 26.4 per cent. At the St. Patrick's Anglican School, 29.2 per cent. At Hermitage, about 31 per cent. At Morne Fendue, 33.8 per cent.

The DPM's 68 votes were concentrated at St. Ann's, where it took 24, and Morne Fendue, where it took 9. Even all of them would not have closed a 687-vote gap.

Turnout and the register

The register grew by 541 names between 2022 and 2026, and 51 more ballots were cast. Turnout fell from 71.3 per cent to 63.8 per cent. Measured against the whole electorate, the NDC's share rose slightly, from 36.9 to 38.8 per cent of all registered voters. The NNP's fell from 33.8 to 23.4 per cent.

Once again the pattern is the same: the NDC held and slightly grew its share of the electorate, while the NNP lost about a third of its support, much of it to non-voting.

Three hundred and forty-four voters

To win the seat back, the NNP needs 344 voters to switch sides, or 688 more of its own supporters to turn out, about two in five of those who stayed home. Recovering the 2022 vote of 1,322 alone would cut the margin by more than 40 per cent.

St. Patrick East was within reach four years ago and can be within reach again. Its history, as the seat of former Prime Minister Tillman Thomas, shows that it rewards long-term representation.

What can be done: a four-year plan for St. Patrick East

St. Patrick East needs the NNP to create the local advantage it lacked in 2026.

Year one, 2027: name the candidate early and commit to that person for two elections. A new candidate in 2026 was simply exposed to the national swing. A candidate known for four years will not be. Open a permanent office in River Sallee, the largest polling site and the NNP's best.

Year two, 2028: build from River Sallee and St. Ann's, where the NNP won divisions, into Hermitage and Morne Fendue. Hold regular meetings with farmers, fishers, young people and families, and publish what they say.

Year three, 2029: recruit a captain for each of the 14 polling divisions. Hold the sitting member to account publicly on what St. Patrick has received from the government.

Year four, 2030: target 344 switchers, starting with the 280 NNP voters lost since 2022.

The lesson for every NNP seat

A new candidate with no time to become known is exposed to the full national swing. St. Patrick East shows exactly that: a 121-vote seat lost by 687 when nothing local stood in the way.

The best protection against a bad national year is a candidate the voters already know. The NNP has four years to give every target seat one.

What it means for 2030/31

  1. A new candidate takes the full national swing: give them time.
  2. Name the candidate in 2027 and keep them for two elections.
  3. Open a permanent office in River Sallee, the NNP's best area.
  4. Build from River Sallee and St. Ann's into Hermitage and Morne Fendue.
  5. Win back the 280 NNP voters lost since 2022.
  6. Captain all 14 divisions.
  7. Hold the sitting member to account on what St. Patrick received.
  8. Target 344 switchers.
