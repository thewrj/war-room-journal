How a Three-Vote Split Cost the NNP the Capital

The NDC didn't ride a wave into the Town of St. George. The opposition divided its own vote, and the NNP's wounds were self-inflicted, opened nearly two years before polling day. Claudette Joseph won with 1,269 votes. Peter David and Troy Noel had 1,266 between them.

The split explains the capital. It doesn't explain a 14–1 national landslide, in which the NDC took 61 per cent of the vote. Several forces beyond the NNP's own troubles were at work.

A record to run on. Analyst Derek Ramsamooj said Prime Minister Dickon Mitchell had shown he could use the office to deliver, and that voters were accepting new, young and dynamic leadership. The government has also pointed to strong economic growth after Hurricane Beryl and record citizenship-by-investment revenue.

The government chose the battlefield. Mr. Mitchell called the vote almost a year early, and the NDC had its campaign planned before the opposition was ready.

The end of an era. This was the NNP's first campaign in decades without Dr. Keith Mitchell at its head. Political scientist Peter Wickham noted that Dickon Mitchell has now beaten both the party's greatest figure and the party he left behind.

A quieter electorate. Turnout fell to 63.3 per cent, down from 70.2 per cent in 2022. In the Town it fell from 65.1 to 60.2 per cent.

The limits of a third party

For Mr. David the problem was structural. Grenada's first-past-the-post system has long been a two-party contest, and anyone running outside the two dominant parties faces steep odds. The DPM drew 1,189 votes nationwide, and its leader lost his own seat.

His history also caught up with him. Mr. David first won the Town for the NDC in 2003 and held it until 2013. A fallout with the Tillman Thomas-led NDC sent him to the NNP, which he represented in the seat in 2018 and 2022. A fallout with the NNP's leadership process led him to found the DPM. Personal popularity did not carry a brand-new party.

Three hundred and sixty-seven voters

Against the NDC alone, the NNP would have needed 367 voters to switch sides to win the Town in 2026, or 734 more supporters to turn out. But the real arithmetic is simpler. The NNP and the DPM together were three votes short. A reunited opposition in the capital starts the next election within touching distance.
