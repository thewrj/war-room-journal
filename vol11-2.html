After Bowen: The Second Test in St. George South East

Gregory Bowen, a former Deputy Prime Minister, lost St. George South East in 2022. In 2026 the NNP tried a new candidate, and the result was worse: a margin of 1,294, no polling divisions, and a fall in the NNP's share faster than the national swing.

The NNP did not carry any of the 20 polling divisions. Its best result was at the Springs Open Bible Church, where it took 39.3 per cent of the two-party vote across two divisions. The Anglican High School division gave it 38.7 per cent, and La Borie, across three divisions, 36.4 per cent.

Its weakest areas were stark. At the Calivigny Community Centre the NNP took 21.8 per cent across two divisions, and in one of them just 26 votes to the NDC's 107. At Hope Vale it took 23.3 per cent. At St. Paul's Anglican Church, across three divisions, 28.7 per cent.

Fewer voters, again

The register grew by 443 names, but 81 fewer ballots were cast. Turnout fell from 70.2 per cent to 63.5 per cent, and 2,137 registered voters did not vote, up from 1,613.

The DPM took 66 votes, one of its better results outside the Town. Even if all of them had gone to the NNP, the margin would still have been more than 1,200. As in most seats outside the Town of St. George, the split was not the problem.

Six hundred and forty-eight voters

To have won the seat, the NNP needed 648 voters to switch sides, or 1,295 more of its own supporters to turn out, three in five of those who stayed home. That is a large gap for one election.

But St. George South East has swung before, and the NNP's 2022 vote of 1,634 is the natural first target. Recovering it would cut the deficit by more than a third and make the seat competitive again by 2030/31.
