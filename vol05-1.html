The Voters Who Stayed Home: How the NNP Lost St. Andrew North East

The NNP held St. Andrew North East in 2008 and again in 2022. In 2026 it lost by 320 votes, and not mainly to switchers. The register grew by 647, but only six more people voted, and the NNP's own vote fell by 339.

St. Andrew North East, which runs from Pearls and La Poterie through Belair, Tivoli and Upper St. John to Paraclete, has been one of the NNP's most dependable seats. Roland Bhola held it in 2008, when the NDC won 11 seats. Kate Lewis-Peters won it in 2018 and held it in 2022, by 2,190 votes to 1,864, when her party lost power.

"If the NNP had simply kept its 2022 voters, it would have won."Greg Hosten

On 5th November she lost it. The NDC's Niecal Joseph took 2,171 votes to Ms. Lewis-Peters's 1,851, with 45 for the DPM's Pamela Williams. A majority of 326 for the NNP four years ago became a deficit of 320.

A sitting member, defeated

This was not a seat lost by a newcomer. Ms. Lewis-Peters, a former secondary school teacher from St. Andrew, had been the member for eight years and a minister in the last NNP government. She was the party's Assistant General Secretary after the December 2024 convention. In January 2025, after Peter David left the NNP, she had to state publicly that she had not resigned from the party.

An incumbent with that profile should be the hardest kind of candidate to beat. Her vote fell by 339. The NDC's rose by only 307. That gap is the story of St. Andrew North East.

The NNP's voters did not switch. They stayed home.

Here is the striking number. Between 2022 and 2026 the register in St. Andrew North East grew by 647 names, from 5,362 to 6,009. The number of ballots cast rose by just six, from 4,071 to 4,077. Turnout fell from 75.9 per cent, one of the highest in the country, to 67.8 per cent.

The NNP lost 339 votes and the NDC gained 307. Even if every one of the NDC's new votes came from former NNP voters, at least 32 of the NNP's 2022 supporters voted for nobody. The more likely reading is larger: with almost no change in total ballots, a substantial part of the NNP's 2022 vote simply did not come out.

That is a different defeat from St. Andrew South East, next door, where the NNP held its vote and the NDC found new voters. Here the NNP's own supporters drifted away, and 1,932 registered voters did not vote, up from 1,291.

Better than the national swing, not good enough

Measured as a share of the two-party vote, the NNP fell from 54.0 per cent to 46.0 per cent, a drop of 8.0 points. Nationally the party fell 10.3 points. Ms. Lewis-Peters ran more than two points ahead of her party's decline, which says something about her personal standing.

But a majority of 326 could not absorb even an eight-point swing. Seats like this one, held in 2022 by a few hundred votes, needed more than a better-than-average result. They needed growth, and there was none.
