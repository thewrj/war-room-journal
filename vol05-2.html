The Voters Who Stayed Home: How the NNP Lost St. Andrew North East

The NNP held St. Andrew North East in 2008 and again in 2022. In 2026 it lost by 320 votes, and not mainly to switchers. The register grew by 647, but only six more people voted, and the NNP's own vote fell by 339.

The polling-division results show a seat split in two. The NNP carried 5 of the 15 divisions. Its strength was in the north: at the Pearls road office it won one division by 175 votes to 114, and it took 54 per cent at Camp Raymond. At La Poterie it won 54.3 per cent across two divisions, including one by a single vote, 107 to 106.

The NDC won the middle and the south of the constituency, and won it clearly. At Tivoli it took 490 votes to 333 across three divisions. At Belair it took 459 to 318. At Paraclete the NNP won only 36 per cent. At Upper St. John, 42 per cent.

The Grenada Christian Academy was split: the NNP won its larger division by 195 to 182 and lost the other by 168 to 127. The DPM's 45 votes were concentrated at La Poterie, where it took 19, in the NNP's best area.

A quiet collapse in turnout

The fall in turnout deserves its own attention. In 2022 St. Andrew North East had one of the highest turnouts in Grenada. In 2026 it was close to the national average. Of all the seats the NNP lost, this one shows most clearly a party that failed to bring its own people to the polls.

There are several possible reasons: a short campaign, a national mood that discouraged opposition supporters, or a branch that had not kept in regular contact with its voters between elections. The numbers cannot say which. They do say where the votes went: not to the NDC, but to the sofa.

One hundred and sixty-one voters

To have held the seat, the NNP needed 161 voters to switch sides, or 321 more of its own supporters to turn out. That is about one in six of the 1,932 who stayed home, and fewer than the 339 votes the NNP lost since 2022.

Put simply, if the NNP had kept its 2022 voters and done nothing else, it would have won. That makes St. Andrew North East a turnout seat first and a persuasion seat second.
