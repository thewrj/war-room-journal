The Crossover That Paid: Delma Thomas Wins Under a New Flag

Delma Thomas won St. Andrew North West for the NNP in 2022, left the party in 2023 and joined the NDC government. In 2026 she won again, by 785 votes, under the other party's colours. Her personal vote went with her.

In 2022 Delma Thomas held St. Andrew North West for the New National Party by 131 votes, 1,898 to 1,767 for the NDC's Gloria Thomas. It was one of only six seats the NNP kept as it lost government.

"A party that cannot keep its own members cannot keep their seats."Greg Hosten

In 2026 Ms. Thomas won the seat again, by 2,242 votes to 1,457, a majority of 785. This time she was the NDC's candidate. The NNP's Marcia Francis finished second, and the DPM's Olvine Holas took 39. A seat the NNP held four years ago is now one of the NDC's safer ones, and it got there by changing sides with its member.

A member who walked

Ms. Thomas was first elected for St. Andrew North West in 2013 and served as a minister in the NNP government. On 8th May 2023 she resigned from the NNP with immediate effect, saying her attempts to represent the constituency from within the party had been frustrated. Two weeks later she announced at a public meeting, alongside Prime Minister Dickon Mitchell, that she would work with the NDC government.

She later joined the Cabinet. The New Today reported that her break with the NNP followed disagreements with Dr. Keith Mitchell over his succession. Whatever the full reasons, the NNP lost a sitting member less than a year after the 2022 election, and with her, the seat.

Her vote went with her

The results show how much of the NNP's 2022 vote in St. Andrew North West belonged to Ms. Thomas personally. The NNP's vote fell by 441, from 1,898 to 1,457. The NDC's rose by 475, from 1,767 to 2,242. The NNP's share of the two-party vote dropped from 51.8 per cent to 39.4 per cent, a fall of 12.4 points, against 10.3 points nationally.

That extra two points is the measure of the crossover. In most seats the NNP fell by around the national swing or less. Here it fell further, because the party's former member was now standing against it and took part of its base with her.

The opposite of the Town of St. George

The contrast with the Town of St. George is instructive. There, Peter David left the NNP, founded his own party and took 730 votes. That split the opposition and handed the seat to the NDC by three votes.

Ms. Thomas did something different. She did not start a new party. She joined the government. Instead of splitting the vote, she brought her supporters into the larger party, and the result was a comfortable win rather than a narrow loss. For a departing NNP member, the second route proved far more effective.

One division left

The polling-division results show how thoroughly the seat changed hands. The NNP carried just 1 of the 16 divisions: the Paraclete Government School, by 99 votes to 80.

Everywhere else it lost, and in some places heavily. At the Paradise Medical Station the NNP won only 30.5 per cent of the two-party vote across two divisions. At Paul's Bakery in Birchgrove it took 33.4 per cent. Even at La Fillette, the largest polling site with four divisions, it managed only 41.7 per cent.

The DPM's 39 votes were concentrated at Sio's Bar in Birchgrove, where it took 26. They did not affect the outcome.
