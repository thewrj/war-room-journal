The Prime Minister's Fortress

The NNP held St. David from 2013 to 2022. Dickon Mitchell took it in 2022 by 1,672 votes and held it in 2026 by 3,180. The NNP did not win a single polling division. Here is what an opposition can and cannot do against a prime minister at home.

What can be done: an eight-year plan for St. David

St. David needs a plan measured in two elections, not one. Its first goal is to narrow the gap, and its second is to be ready when the seat opens.

Years one and two, 2027 to 2028: confirm a candidate who will commit to St. David for at least two elections, so that voters see continuity rather than a new name every cycle. Open a permanent office. Begin with Marlmount and Pomme Rose, where the NNP is strongest, and build outwards.

Years three and four, 2029 to 2030: recruit a captain for every polling division. Hold the Prime Minister to account in public on what St. David itself has received, measured against his promises. Set realistic targets: win back the 604 NNP votes lost since 2022, and lift the party's share above 35 per cent.

Beyond 2030: keep the office open and the candidate in place. The NNP held St. David for nine years until 2022. Seats that change hands with a leader can change back when the leader goes, but only for a party that never left.

The lesson for every NNP seat

Not every seat is winnable in one election, and a serious party says so plainly. St. David is the clearest example in this series. But a seat that cannot be won in 2030 can still add votes to the national total, train volunteers, and prepare for the day it opens.

The NNP's recent habit has been to name caretakers late in seats it does not expect to win. In St. David that produced a candidate with 19 months against a prime minister. The alternative is to treat hard seats as long-term investments, with the same candidate and the same office for eight years.

What it means for 2030/31

  1. Be honest about hard seats: St. David cannot be won in one election.
  2. Contest it anyway: every vote counts toward the national share.
  3. Commit to one candidate for two elections, not a new name each cycle.
  4. Name caretakers early: 19 months is too short against a prime minister.
  5. Build out from Marlmount and Pomme Rose, the NNP's best ground.
  6. Win back the 604 NNP votes lost since 2022.
  7. Keep a permanent office open between elections.
  8. Prepare for the day the seat opens: leaders do not stay forever.
