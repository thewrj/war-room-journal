How a Three-Vote Split Cost the NNP the Capital

The NDC didn't ride a wave into the Town of St. George. The opposition divided its own vote, and the NNP's wounds were self-inflicted, opened nearly two years before polling day. Claudette Joseph won with 1,269 votes. Peter David and Troy Noel had 1,266 between them.

By Greg HostenNov. 7, 2026 · 6 min read

Political commentators expected Peter David to emerge from the 5 November general election as a kingmaker in the capital. Instead the veteran politician, running for the first time under the banner of his new Democratic People's Movement, polled 730 votes. Attorney General Claudette Joseph of the governing NDC took the Town of St. George with 1,269. The NNP's candidate, Troy Noel, drew 536.

"This was a split-opposition loss, not a rejection of the NNP in town."Greg Hosten

Put the two opposition tallies together and they reach 1,266: three votes short of the winner. Not every David voter would have backed an NNP candidate, and a share of his support crossed party lines. But the arithmetic tells the story of the night in the capital. This was a split-opposition loss, not a rejection of the NNP in town.

A small seat, where every household can be reached

The Town of St. George is one of the smallest constituencies in the country. This year it had 4,242 registered voters, and 2,555 ballots were cast. About 1,300 votes wins it. That means a determined candidate can personally reach almost every household in the seat, and a divided party can lose it on a handful of streets.

A party divided

The NNP's troubles began at its own convention. Mr. David was one of only two contenders for the party leadership in December 2024. He withdrew hours before the vote on 15 December, saying the process had been rigged in favour of Emmalin Pierre. On 20 January 2025, a little over a month after the party elected its new executive, he resigned from the NNP.

Losing a leadership race became losing a sitting member of Parliament, then his base, and finally the seat. The split never healed. Mr. David went on to found the Democratic People's Movement and lead it into the 2026 election.

A branch that collapsed

The damage reached the constituency machine within days. That same January the NNP's General Secretary confirmed that several executive members of its Town of St. George constituency group had resigned, leaving the party without a working local organisation in the capital for most of the cycle.

In a seat this small, the branch is the campaign. Without one, the NNP entered the 2026 election in the capital with no organisation to find its voters, no canvass list and no one to run polling day.

A candidate too late

When the NNP finally named a flag-bearer for the capital, it chose Troy Noel, who is originally from the Darbeau area of the constituency but now lives at Hope Vale, in St. George South East. A candidate who lived outside the seat, named late in the cycle, had little time to build the trust that a capital constituency rewards.

The result was the worst NNP performance anywhere in the country. The party's vote in the Town fell from 1,329 in 2022 to 536, a loss of 793. Its share of the vote for the two main parties fell 24.6 points, more than twice the national swing.

Where the split happened

The polling-division results show the split street by street. The DPM finished ahead of the NNP in 10 of the 12 divisions. The NNP beat the DPM only at the two St. George's Anglican Senior School divisions.

Combined, the NNP and the DPM carried all five divisions at the National Stadium, the largest polling site, by 779 votes to the NDC's 609. The NDC won the seat in the older parts of town: at the Berean Christian Academy by 148 to 80 for the two opposition parties combined, at Norton's Hall on Church Street by 92 to 62, and at the Magistrate's Court by 52 to 33.

At Marryshow House the NDC won by 69 votes to 67 for the NNP and DPM together. At the St. George's SDA School it won by 123 to 119. Those two divisions alone were decided by six votes.

Persistence pays for the NDC

Ms. Joseph's victory came on her third attempt. In 2018 Mr. David, then the NNP's candidate, beat her by 1,616 votes to 789. In 2022 he beat her again, by 1,329 to 1,118. She kept showing up.

Her vote in 2026, 1,269, was only 151 more than in 2022. She did not need to grow much. She needed the opposition to divide, and it did. Her share of all registered voters barely moved, from 29.6 to 29.9 per cent. The NNP's fell from 35.1 to 12.6 per cent.
