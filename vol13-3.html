From 121 Votes: Two New Faces in St. Patrick East

St. Patrick East was the NDC's narrowest win in 2022 after St. John, decided by 121 votes. In 2026 both parties changed candidates. The NDC's newcomer grew the party's vote; the NNP's fell by 280, and the margin rose to 687.

What can be done: a four-year plan for St. Patrick East

St. Patrick East needs the NNP to create the local advantage it lacked in 2026.

Year one, 2027: name the candidate early and commit to that person for two elections. A new candidate in 2026 was simply exposed to the national swing. A candidate known for four years will not be. Open a permanent office in River Sallee, the largest polling site and the NNP's best.

Year two, 2028: build from River Sallee and St. Ann's, where the NNP won divisions, into Hermitage and Morne Fendue. Hold regular meetings with farmers, fishers, young people and families, and publish what they say.

Year three, 2029: recruit a captain for each of the 14 polling divisions. Hold the sitting member to account publicly on what St. Patrick has received from the government.

Year four, 2030: target 344 switchers, starting with the 280 NNP voters lost since 2022.

The lesson for every NNP seat

A new candidate with no time to become known is exposed to the full national swing. St. Patrick East shows exactly that: a 121-vote seat lost by 687 when nothing local stood in the way.

The best protection against a bad national year is a candidate the voters already know. The NNP has four years to give every target seat one.

What it means for 2030/31

  1. A new candidate takes the full national swing: give them time.
  2. Name the candidate in 2027 and keep them for two elections.
  3. Open a permanent office in River Sallee, the NNP's best area.
  4. Build from River Sallee and St. Ann's into Hermitage and Morne Fendue.
  5. Win back the 280 NNP voters lost since 2022.
  6. Captain all 14 divisions.
  7. Hold the sitting member to account on what St. Patrick received.
  8. Target 344 switchers.
