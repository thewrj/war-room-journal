The Seat That Survived 2008: How the NNP Lost St. Mark

St. Mark stayed with the NNP when the party was routed in 2008 and again when it lost power in 2022. In 2026, with a new candidate on the ballot, its two-party share fell 14 points, faster than the country, and the NDC took the seat by 261 votes.

By Greg Hosten26th November 2026 · 5 min read
WhatsAppXFacebook

St. Mark is small. It had 4,200 registered voters this year, the fewest of any constituency in the country. That is precisely why it matters. In a small seat a few hundred voters decide everything, and a party that is present every week can hold it against the national mood, as the NNP did in 2008.

The danger for the NNP is that small seats are easy to take for granted. A loyal member masks the state of the branch, and the branch is only tested when the member leaves. In 2026 St. Mark was tested, and the result suggests the party organisation underneath had not been kept in the same condition as the member's personal standing.

One hundred and thirty-one voters

The arithmetic of a comeback is modest. To have held the seat, the NNP needed 131 voters to switch sides, or 262 more of its own supporters to turn out. That second figure is about one in five of the 1,373 registered voters who did not vote.

Few seats the NNP lost in 2026 can be won back with so few votes. Only St. Andrew South East, where the party needs 82 switchers, is closer. St. Mark should be near the top of any NNP target list for 2030/31.
