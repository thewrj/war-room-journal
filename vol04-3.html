The Seat That Survived 2008: How the NNP Lost St. Mark

St. Mark stayed with the NNP when the party was routed in 2008 and again when it lost power in 2022. In 2026, with a new candidate on the ballot, its two-party share fell 14 points, faster than the country, and the NDC took the seat by 261 votes.

What can be done: a four-year plan for St. Mark

St. Mark was won by the NDC with new voters and an even spread of small gains. It can be won back the same way, but only by starting early.

Year one, 2027: name the candidate early and commit to that person for the next election, so that St. Mark's voters have four years, not four months, to know them. Reopen a permanent office in Victoria. Build a household list for each of the 13 polling divisions, starting with the tied Victoria division and the two Duquesne divisions.

Year two, 2028: run a listening programme across the parish's main groups: fishers and vendors in Victoria, farmers on the hillsides, young people and returning nationals. Publish what they say, and build a short local programme from it. Register new voters every month. The register grew by 510 in four years, and the NDC found most of them.

Year three, 2029: recruit a captain for each of the 13 divisions, with a target number of household contacts each quarter. Put particular effort into Samaritan, where the NNP took under a third of the vote, because a seat this small cannot be won while one community is written off.

Year four, 2030: campaign on numbers within reach. Hold St. Mark's Secondary School and Duquesne, win back the Victoria divisions lost by a handful of votes each, and narrow Samaritan. The target is 131 switchers or 262 new supporters.

The lesson for every NNP seat

A loyal seat is loyal to someone. In St. Mark the NNP's strength rested on a sitting member who had held the seat through two NDC waves. When she left the ballot, the NNP's share fell faster than the national swing.

The method that worked in St. George North West was an early, public handover and a successor with time to become known. St. Mark shows the cost of not having that. Every NNP seat with a long-serving member, now or in the future, should plan its succession years in advance.

What it means for 2030/31

  1. Plan the succession: a loyal seat is loyal to its member, not automatically to the party.
  2. Name new candidates early and in public, as in St. George North West.
  3. Watch the register: it grew by 510, and the NDC found most of the new voters.
  4. Fight for every division: the NDC won with small gains spread everywhere.
  5. Do not write off Samaritan: in a seat this small, every community counts.
  6. Reopen a permanent office in Victoria, the parish capital.
  7. Captain all 13 divisions, starting with the tied Victoria division.
  8. Target 131 switchers: one of the smallest gaps of any lost seat.
