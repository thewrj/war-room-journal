The Leader Who Lost Her Own Seat

Emmalin Pierre lost St. Andrew South East by 162 votes. She did not lose her own voters: her vote fell by just 44. She lost the race to find new ones, to a man she had beaten in 2022 who never left the constituency.

GH
By Greg HostenNov. 19, 2026 · 6 min read
WhatsAppXFacebook

On election night, the New National Party lost more than a government it had hoped to form. It lost its leader's seat. In St. Andrew South East, Education Minister David Andrew took 2,011 votes to 1,849 for Emmalin Pierre, the party's political leader and the woman who hoped to become Grenada's first female prime minister. The DPM's Shirley McQueen took 19. A margin of 162 votes ended a 13-year hold.

"She did not lose her voters. Her vote fell by 44. The NDC found 351 more."Greg Hosten

The defeat is usually told as part of the national landslide. The numbers tell a more specific story, and a more useful one for 2030/31. Ms. Pierre kept almost all of her 2022 voters. The NDC simply found more of its own.

A seat that was slipping for years

St. Andrew South East, which takes in Grenville, Telescope, Soubise, Marquis, Crochu and Grand Bacolet, has never been safe for anyone. Ms. Pierre lost it to the NDC's Patrick Simmons by 30 votes in 2008, won it back in 2013, and beat Mr. Simmons again in 2018 by 2,292 votes to 1,134, almost 67 per cent of the two-party vote.

Then the margin began to fall. In 2022 she beat David Andrew by 1,893 to 1,660, a majority of 233. Her share of the two-party vote dropped to 53.3 per cent. In four years a majority of 1,158 had shrunk by four-fifths. That was the warning. The party read it as a win.

The leader in a marginal seat

Two years later the NNP chose Ms. Pierre as its political leader, at a convention on 15 December 2024 that was about two years overdue. Her rival, Peter David, withdrew hours before the vote, saying the process had been rigged, and left the party the following month. In March 2025 she became Leader of the Opposition.

That put the party's leader, its national spokesperson and its main campaigner in a seat with a 233-vote majority. A party leader must spend the campaign everywhere. A marginal seat needs its candidate at home. In 2026 the NNP asked one person to do both, and gave the seat no visible plan to cover her absence.

The man who stayed

Mr. Andrew is a counselling therapist and educator who had been a classroom teacher, guidance counsellor and Chief Education Officer. He lost to Ms. Pierre in 2022. He did not move on. He went on to serve as Minister of Education, and when the next election came he was back on the same ballot, in the same seat, against the same opponent.

Set that beside St. George South, where the NNP has run four different candidates in four elections. The NDC's lesson from St. Andrew South East is that a losing candidate who stays becomes a winning candidate. Four years of being known is worth more than any campaign.

She kept her voters. They found more.

Here is the most important number in this volume. Between 2022 and 2026, Ms. Pierre's vote fell by only 44, from 1,893 to 1,849. Mr. Andrew's rose by 351, from 1,660 to 2,011. The NNP did not lose this seat to switchers. It lost it to new and returning NDC voters.

The register grew by 557 names over the four years, and 318 more ballots were cast. Almost every extra vote went to the NDC. The NNP held its base and stood still. In a growing constituency, standing still is losing.

Measured against the whole electorate, the picture is the same. In 2022 Ms. Pierre won the votes of 38.9 per cent of all registered voters, and Mr. Andrew 34.1 per cent. In 2026 her share fell to 34.1 per cent, and his rose to 37.1 per cent. The two candidates traded places almost exactly.

She outran her party, and still lost

None of this means Ms. Pierre ran a weak race in her own seat. Her share of the two-party vote fell 5.4 points, from 53.3 to 47.9 per cent. Nationally the NNP fell 10.3 points. She ran more than ten points ahead of her party, which is a sign of personal standing in St. Andrew South East.

But a 233-vote majority could not absorb even half the national swing. With the party's national vote falling as far as it did, the seat was lost the moment the NNP failed to grow its own vote there. The national campaign did not give her enough, and the local campaign did not find what was missing.

Seven divisions, and the knife edge

The polling-division results show a seat split almost down the middle. The NNP carried 7 of the 16 divisions. Its strongest ground was Grand Bacolet, where it won 60.8 per cent of the two-party vote across two divisions, and one Grand Bacolet division by 123 to 62. It also held Mamma Cannes and narrowly carried Telescope and Marquis.

The NDC won the seat in Grenville and its surroundings. At St. Andrew's Methodist School it took about 60 per cent across two divisions. At Fort View, Mount Carmel, it did the same. At the Crochu R.C. School it won 2 of 3 divisions.

Several divisions were decided by a handful of votes. The NNP won one Telescope division by 3 votes and Marquis by 8. It lost Post Royal by 9 and one Crochu division by 14. In a seat lost by 162, every one of those counts.

Eighty-two voters

The most important number for the future is this. To have held the seat, the NNP needed 82 voters to switch sides. That is about five people in each of the 16 polling divisions. Or it needed 163 more of its own supporters to turn out, about one in ten of the 1,536 who stayed home.

Turnout here was 71.7 per cent, the highest in the country. These voters are engaged. They came out, they listened, and a few more of them were persuaded by the other side. This is a seat that can be won back by organisation and presence, with no change in the national mood at all.

A campaign that never caught fire

The national picture explains part of the loss. The NDC called the election nearly a year before it was due, giving the opposition little more than a month of formal campaign. The NNP's offer, including a promise to remove VAT on sugar within 90 days, did not cut through. After the results, the Barbados-based political scientist Peter Wickham said Ms. Pierre should consider stepping down to allow the party to refresh itself.

Whether or not one agrees, the point for St. Andrew South East is narrower. A leader's seat should be the best-run campaign in the party, with its own manager, its own volunteers and its own budget, so that the leader can travel. There is no public sign that this seat had any of that.

What can be done: a four-year plan for the south-east

St. Andrew South East is the most winnable seat the NNP lost. It needs 82 switchers, not a political earthquake. The steps follow from the numbers.

Year one, 2027: decide early, and in public, who the candidate will be, whether Ms. Pierre or a successor, and commit to that person through to polling day. If the candidate is also the party leader, appoint a full-time constituency manager and a deputy who campaigns locally whenever the leader is away. Open a permanent office in Grenville.

Year two, 2028: concentrate on Grenville itself. The St. Andrew's Methodist School and Fort View divisions gave the NDC about 60 per cent. Hold regular meetings with Grenville's vendors, bus operators, fishers in Soubise and young families, and build a short local programme from what they say. Register new voters every month, because the register grew by 557 in four years and the NDC found most of them.

Year three, 2029: give each of the 16 polling divisions a captain, with special attention to the knife-edge divisions at Telescope, Marquis, Post Royal and Crochu. Hold the sitting member to account in public, measured against the government's own promises for St. Andrew.

Year four, 2030: campaign on numbers that are within reach. Hold Grand Bacolet and Mamma Cannes, win back Telescope and Crochu, narrow the gap in Grenville, and find 82 switchers or 163 new supporters. No other lost NNP seat offers a target this small.

The lesson for every NNP seat

A shrinking majority is a warning, not a win. The NNP held St. Andrew South East in 2022 by 233 votes and treated the result as safe enough to make its member the party leader. Four years later the NDC had found 351 new votes, and the leader was out of Parliament.

The NDC's method here was simple: keep the losing candidate, give him a public role, and spend four years adding voters. The NNP can use the same method, starting with the seat where it needs the fewest votes of all.

What it means for 2030/31

  1. A shrinking majority is a warning: 1,158 became 233, then a defeat.
  2. A leader in a marginal seat needs a full-time local campaign behind her.
  3. Keep a losing candidate who stays: the NDC did, and won.
  4. Holding the base is not enough when the register is growing.
  5. Register new voters every month: the NDC found most of the 557.
  6. Win Grenville, or at least narrow it: it decided this seat.
  7. Captain the knife-edge divisions: Telescope, Marquis, Post Royal, Crochu.
  8. Target 82 switchers: the smallest gap of any seat the NNP lost.
