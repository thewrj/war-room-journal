The Rematch in St. George North East

Ron Redhead and Nimrod Ollivierre met for the second election running. In 2022 Mr. Redhead won by 1,087. In 2026 he won by 2,027, and the NNP did not carry a single one of 23 polling divisions. The NNP lost 585 votes, and 230 fewer people voted at all.

The NNP did not carry any of the 23 polling divisions. Its best results were in the smallest: 44.5 per cent at the Constantine Methodist School and 40.3 per cent across two divisions at Vendomme. At Vendomme the NNP came within ten votes in one division, 74 to 64.

The large population centres went heavily to the NDC. At St. Mary's R.C. School in Tempe, with five divisions and more than 2,000 voters, the NNP took 31.0 per cent. At Mount Parnassus, 29.6 per cent. At St. Paul's, 26.9 per cent. At La Mode, 26.5 per cent, including one division the NDC won by 172 votes to 32. At Boca Secondary School the NNP took 23.0 per cent.

What a rematch tells you

A rematch is a natural experiment. When the same two candidates meet again, the result measures what each side did in between. In St. George North East, Mr. Redhead held his share of the electorate. The NNP lost about a third of its vote.

Running the same losing candidate again can work, as David Andrew showed in St. Andrew South East. But it works only when the candidate spends the intervening years visibly working the seat. The numbers here do not show that kind of growth on the NNP side.

One thousand and fourteen voters

To have won the seat, the NNP would have needed 1,014 voters to switch sides, or 2,028 more of its own supporters to turn out, almost three in five of those who stayed home. St. George North East is not a seat the NNP can expect to win in 2030 on a normal swing.

It is, however, a large seat of 8,617 voters that the NNP held as recently as 2018. Restoring the party's 2022 vote alone would cut the NDC's majority by more than a quarter, and add hundreds of votes to the NNP's national total.
