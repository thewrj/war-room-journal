After Bowen: The Second Test in St. George South East

Gregory Bowen, a former Deputy Prime Minister, lost St. George South East in 2022. In 2026 the NNP tried a new candidate, and the result was worse: a margin of 1,294, no polling divisions, and a fall in the NNP's share faster than the national swing.

What can be done: a four-year plan for the south-east

St. George South East needs a candidate with time to become known, and a party that treats the seat as winnable again.

Year one, 2027: choose the candidate early and commit to that person for two elections. If Mr. Horsford stands again, fund an office and an organiser. Ask Mr. Bowen and other former members of the branch to help introduce the candidate across the constituency: a public handover, even years late, is better than none.

Year two, 2028: build out from Springs, La Borie and the Anglican High School area, where the NNP did best. Hold regular meetings in Morne Jaloux and the Marian area, and with young families and workers along the Calivigny corridor. Register new voters every month.

Year three, 2029: recruit a captain for each of the 20 divisions. Give special attention to Calivigny, Hope Vale and St. Paul's, where the NNP fell below 30 per cent, because no seat can be won while whole communities are written off.

Year four, 2030: set targets in order. Win the first polling division. Restore the 2022 vote of 1,634. Bring the NNP's share of the two-party vote back above 40 per cent. Those three steps would put the seat back in play.

The lesson for every NNP seat

When a long-serving member loses, the party has two choices: rebuild around them or rebuild around someone new. Either can work. What does not work is a new candidate named late, without the old member's visible support and without time to build a base.

St. George South East, like St. Mark and St. George North West, shows that how the NNP handles succession decides seats. The party should write down its succession rules and apply them in every constituency, starting now.

What it means for 2030/31

  1. Plan succession: a new name, named late, fell faster than the national swing.
  2. Use former members to introduce new candidates in public.
  3. A swing seat swings towards the party that is present.
  4. Build out from Springs and La Borie, the NNP's best ground.
  5. Do not write off Calivigny, Hope Vale or St. Paul's.
  6. Register new voters monthly: 443 joined the roll, fewer people voted.
  7. Captain all 20 divisions and win the first one.
  8. Restore the 2022 vote of 1,634 as the first milestone.
