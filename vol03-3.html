The Leader Who Lost Her Own Seat

Emmalin Pierre lost St. Andrew South East by 162 votes. She did not lose her own voters: her vote fell by just 44. She lost the race to find new ones, to a man she had beaten in 2022 who never left the constituency.

What can be done: a four-year plan for the south-east

St. Andrew South East is the most winnable seat the NNP lost. It needs 82 switchers, not a political earthquake. The steps follow from the numbers.

Year one, 2027: decide early, and in public, who the candidate will be, whether Ms. Pierre or a successor, and commit to that person through to polling day. If the candidate is also the party leader, appoint a full-time constituency manager and a deputy who campaigns locally whenever the leader is away. Open a permanent office in Grenville.

Year two, 2028: concentrate on Grenville itself. The St. Andrew's Methodist School and Fort View divisions gave the NDC about 60 per cent. Hold regular meetings with Grenville's vendors, bus operators, fishers in Soubise and young families, and build a short local programme from what they say. Register new voters every month, because the register grew by 557 in four years and the NDC found most of them.

Year three, 2029: give each of the 16 polling divisions a captain, with special attention to the knife-edge divisions at Telescope, Marquis, Post Royal and Crochu. Hold the sitting member to account in public, measured against the government's own promises for St. Andrew.

Year four, 2030: campaign on numbers that are within reach. Hold Grand Bacolet and Mamma Cannes, win back Telescope and Crochu, narrow the gap in Grenville, and find 82 switchers or 163 new supporters. No other lost NNP seat offers a target this small.

The lesson for every NNP seat

A shrinking majority is a warning, not a win. The NNP held St. Andrew South East in 2022 by 233 votes and treated the result as safe enough to make its member the party leader. Four years later the NDC had found 351 new votes, and the leader was out of Parliament.

The NDC's method here was simple: keep the losing candidate, give him a public role, and spend four years adding voters. The NNP can use the same method, starting with the seat where it needs the fewest votes of all.

What it means for 2030/31

  1. A shrinking majority is a warning: 1,158 became 233, then a defeat.
  2. A leader in a marginal seat needs a full-time local campaign behind her.
  3. Keep a losing candidate who stays: the NDC did, and won.
  4. Holding the base is not enough when the register is growing.
  5. Register new voters every month: the NDC found most of the 557.
  6. Win Grenville, or at least narrow it: it decided this seat.
  7. Captain the knife-edge divisions: Telescope, Marquis, Post Royal, Crochu.
  8. Target 82 switchers: the smallest gap of any seat the NNP lost.
