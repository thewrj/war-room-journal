Every Polling Station: The Sister Isles After Beryl

Hurricane Beryl flattened Carriacou and Petite Martinique in July 2024. Two years later the sister isles re-elected the NDC's Tevin Andrews by 976 votes over the NNP's deputy leader. The NNP carried one polling division, in its candidate's home village.

The polling-division results are striking. The NNP carried just 1 of the 18 divisions: one of the two at the Dover Government School, Mr. Cox's home village, by 74 votes to 69. It lost the other Dover division by 105 to 46.

Its next-best results were at the Windward Medical Station, where it lost by 136 to 123, and at L'Esterre, where it took 43.1 per cent across three divisions. Petite Martinique, the island hardest hit by Beryl, gave the NNP 38.3 per cent across two divisions.

Hillsborough, the main town, went heavily to the NDC. At the Hillsborough Government School the NNP took 20.3 per cent across two divisions, and in one of them just 30 votes to 174. At Mount Pleasant it took 24.6 per cent. At Bogles, 28.8 per cent.

Turnout and the register

The register grew by 596 names, and 45 more ballots were cast. Turnout fell from 69.8 per cent to 63.6 per cent, and 2,185 registered voters did not vote. The NDC's share of the whole electorate rose from 36.1 to 39.8 per cent. The NNP's fell from 33.3 to 23.6 per cent.

Some of the fall in turnout may reflect people still displaced after Beryl. But the shift between the parties is clear, and it ran towards the government that led the rebuilding.

Four hundred and eighty-nine voters

To win the seat back, the NNP needs 489 voters to switch sides, or 977 more of its own supporters to turn out, about 45 per cent of those who stayed home. Recovering the 2022 vote of 1,805 alone would cut the margin by 40 per cent.

Carriacou and Petite Martinique has a long NNP history and was lost by only 149 votes four years ago. It is winnable, but only by a party that commits to the islands as a constituency in their own right.
