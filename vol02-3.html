The Last Fortress: How the NNP Held St. George North West

The NNP's only surviving seat held because the party planned a succession, chose early and stayed united. Yet the fortress is shrinking: fewer voters, a lost village, and a vote that has declined at every election since 2018.

By Greg Hosten12th November 2026 · 7 min read

What can be done: a four-year plan to hold and grow

St. George North West does not need rescuing. It needs a sitting member who converts an inherited majority into a personal one, and a party that uses its only seat as a base from which to rebuild the rest of St. George.

Year one, 2027: open a permanent constituency office and publish regular surgery hours, so that Dr. Joseph becomes known for his own service and not only for Dr. Mitchell's endorsement. Begin in Willis and Grenville Vale, where the NNP is weakest, before turning to Brizan and Happy Hill, where it is strongest. Build a household list of the 2,027 who stayed home.

Year two, 2028: run a turnout programme aimed at young and first-time voters, whose names are swelling the register even as the number of ballots falls. Register new voters every month rather than every election, and give each of the 15 polling divisions its own captain, with a quarterly target for contacts.

Year three, 2029: make the seat the NNP's training ground for St. George. Send volunteers from Brizan, Happy Hill and Grand Mal to help rebuild the branches in the Town of St. George and St. George North East, where the party's vote collapsed. A fortress that does not supply the rest of the army is merely a refuge.

Year four, 2030: set targets that measure growth rather than survival. Win back Willis, lift turnout above 66 per cent again, and push the NNP vote back above the 2,211 Dr. Mitchell won in 2022. If all three are achieved, the seat will be safer at the next election than it was at this one, which has not been true since 2018.

The lesson for every NNP seat

Here the party chose early, the outgoing member handed over in public, the branch stayed united, and the split hurt the other side. In the Town of St. George every one of those went the wrong way, and the NNP lost the capital by three votes.

The method is no secret. It was tried in one constituency in 2026, and it worked amid the worst national result in the party's history. The question for 2030/31 is whether the NNP will apply it in the other fourteen, beginning now.

What it means for 2030/31

  1. Choose early: ten months of caretaking beat eight weeks of campaigning.
  2. Hand over in public: a trusted incumbent's endorsement transfers loyalty.
  3. Holding is not growing: the NNP vote here has fallen at every election since 2018.
  4. Fix the weak spots first: Willis and Grenville Vale before the strongholds.
  5. Chase the 2,027 non-voters: turnout fell seven points in four years.
  6. Turn an inherited majority into a personal one through visible service.
  7. Use safe seats to train volunteers for the seats that were lost.
  8. A breakaway hurts only when the base walks out with the candidate.
