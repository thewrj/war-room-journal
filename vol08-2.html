The Prime Minister's Fortress

The NNP held St. David from 2013 to 2022. Dickon Mitchell took it in 2022 by 1,672 votes and held it in 2026 by 3,180. The NNP did not win a single polling division. Here is what an opposition can and cannot do against a prime minister at home.

By Greg HostenDec. 24, 2026 · 4 min read
WhatsAppXFacebook

St. David's register grew by 1,444 names between 2022 and 2026, the largest increase of any seat. Only 293 more ballots were cast, and turnout fell from 72.9 to 66.2 per cent. This year 3,828 registered voters did not vote.

The NDC added 904 votes. The NNP lost 604. Measured against the whole electorate, the NDC won the votes of 47.0 per cent of all registered voters, and the NNP 18.9 per cent. Nearly half the electorate actively voted for the Prime Minister.

What the numbers say is possible

To have won St. David, the NNP would have needed 1,591 voters to switch sides, or 3,181 new supporters, more than four in five of everyone who stayed home. That is not achievable in one election against a sitting prime minister, and a serious plan should say so.

But a seat that cannot be won can still be contested. Every vote the NNP adds in St. David counts in its national share, which fell to 37.7 per cent this year. A constituency of 11,310 voters is too large to abandon. And prime ministers do not stay in office forever: when Mr. Mitchell eventually steps down, St. David will be an open seat, and the party that has been present throughout will be best placed to take it.
