The Rematch in St. George North East

Ron Redhead and Nimrod Ollivierre met for the second election running. In 2022 Mr. Redhead won by 1,087. In 2026 he won by 2,027, and the NNP did not carry a single one of 23 polling divisions. The NNP lost 585 votes, and 230 fewer people voted at all.

Rematches are rare in Grenadian politics, and they are revealing. In St. George North East, the NDC's Ron Redhead and the NNP's Nimrod Ollivierre faced each other in 2022 and again in 2026. The same two names, the same seat, four years apart. Whatever changed, changed in the voters and in the campaigns, not on the ballot.

"A rematch measures what each side did in between. Here, the NNP lost a third of its vote."Greg Hosten

In 2022 Mr. Redhead won by 3,220 votes to 2,133. On 5th November he won by 3,575 to 1,548, with 19 for the GSDP and 4 for the GRP. His majority almost doubled, from 1,087 to 2,027.

A seat with a complicated history

St. George North East takes in Tempe, Mount Parnassus, Beaulieu, La Mode, Boca, New Hampshire, Vendomme and the St. Paul's area. It has changed hands more than once. The NDC's Nazim Burke held it after 2008. The NNP's Tobias Clement won it in 2013 and 2018, then left the government side and became Leader of the Opposition in the last Parliament, leaving the NNP to find a new caretaker.

That caretaker was Mr. Ollivierre, who lost in 2022 when the NDC took government. Mr. Redhead, a former youth senator and long-standing NDC member, won the seat and entered government. Four years later both men were back.

The NNP fell; the NDC held

The NNP's vote fell by 585, from 2,133 to 1,548. The NDC's rose by 355. The NNP's share of the two-party vote dropped from 39.8 per cent to 30.2 per cent, a fall of 9.6 points, close to the national swing of 10.3.

What stands out is the shape of the change. Mr. Redhead's share of the whole electorate barely moved, from 41.7 per cent of all registered voters to 41.5 per cent. The NNP's fell from 27.6 per cent to 18.0 per cent. The NDC did not grow its base so much as keep it, while the NNP's support drained away.

Fewer voters than four years ago

St. George North East's register grew by 890 names between 2022 and 2026. Yet 230 fewer ballots were cast. Turnout fell from 69.8 per cent to 59.9 per cent, a drop of 9.9 points, tied with St. George South for the largest fall in the country.

This year 3,457 registered voters did not vote, up from 2,337. With the NDC's vote stable and the total falling, most of those new non-voters came from people who had supported the NNP or who would have been its natural supporters. A candidate the voters had already seen lose once did not give them a new reason to come out.
