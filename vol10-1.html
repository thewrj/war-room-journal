The Revolving Door: How the NNP Gave Away Grenada's Biggest Seat

St. George South holds the airport, Grand Anse, the university and most of the hotels. The NNP has sent it four candidates in four elections, lost it by 2,792 votes, and failed to win a single polling division. Here is how it could start again.

GH
By Greg Hosten7th January 2027 · 8 min read
WhatsAppXFacebook

No constituency in Grenada matters more to the economy than St. George South, and none has been handled more carelessly by the New National Party. It is the country's largest seat, with 12,103 registered voters this year, nearly three times the Town of St. George. It takes in Grand Anse, True Blue, L'Anse aux Épines, Calliste, Woburn and Springs, along with the international airport at Point Salines, St. George's University and most of the island's major hotels. On 5th November the NDC's Andy Williams took 4,637 votes to 1,845 for the NNP's Christopher DeAllie Jr. The NNP has treated this seat as a posting. Mr. Williams has treated it as home.

"A full slate is a press release. An office, a staff, a canvass list and a budget are a campaign."Greg Hosten

Four elections, four candidates

Look at who the NNP has put on the ballot here. In 2013 it was Alexandra Otway-Noel. In 2018 it was Nickolas Steele. In 2022 it was Norland Cox. In 2026 it was Christopher DeAllie Jr. That is four different candidates in four consecutive elections, in the one seat where name recognition is hardest to build because the electorate is so large.

Each change reset the clock. Voters in Grand Anse and Calliste never got to know one NNP representative over several terms, and the party never gave a candidate the time to become part of the furniture. In politics, the absence of a familiar face is itself a message, and the message here was that the seat could be filled from anywhere.

Nine years in power bought no loyalty

The NNP held St. George South for the whole of its nine years in government, from 2013 to 2022. It had ministers, budgets and the airport, the beach and the hotel belt under its administration. It lost the seat at the first election after it lost power, and in 2026 it lost it by more than two and a half times the 2022 margin.

That is the sharpest indictment in this volume. The NNP's hold on the south was a hold of incumbency, not of organisation. Mr. Steele's 3,536 votes in 2018 were the party's high point, and the party appears to have mistaken a sitting minister's personal vote for a party base. When the minister retired and the government changed, there was nothing underneath. Since 2018 the NNP's vote here has fallen from 3,536 to 1,845. It has lost almost half its voters in eight years.

2022: the minister from Carriacou

The clearest example came in 2022. With Mr. Steele retiring, the NNP branch endorsed Norland Cox at the end of January as its candidate. Mr. Cox was the Infrastructure Development Minister and a senator, and he was from Carriacou. The party was, in effect, running a sister-isle minister against a Grand Anse businessman in Grand Anse.

The NDC's choice was Andy Williams, founder of the Hot Spot supermarket chain and a former financial comptroller at the Rex Grenadian hotel. He was local, known and visible. On polling day he took 4,029 votes to Mr. Cox's 2,961, a margin of 1,068.

Mr. Cox did not stay to fight again. In September 2025 the NNP named him caretaker for Carriacou and Petite Martinique, where he lost again this year, 2,392 votes to 1,416. The party moved its 2022 candidate to a different seat and lost both.

The numbers behind the collapse

This is not one bad night. It is two in a row. Between 2018 and 2022 the NNP's share of the two-party vote here fell from 56.9 per cent to 42.4 per cent, a drop of 14.5 points. Between 2022 and 2026 it fell again, to 28.5 per cent, a further 13.9 points, against a national fall of 10.3 points. The south turned on the NNP more sharply than the country did.

The make-up of the change matters. In 2022 the NDC gained 1,351 votes while the NNP lost only 575, so more than half of Mr. Williams's new votes came from people who had not backed either party in 2018. In 2026 the pattern reversed. The NNP lost 1,116 votes and the NDC gained only 608. Even if every one of the NDC's new votes came from NNP switchers, at least 508 of the NNP's 2022 voters simply did not vote for anyone. The NNP is no longer only losing the argument here. It is losing its own people to the sofa.

Measured against the whole electorate, the picture is starker. Mr. Williams won the votes of 38 per cent of every registered voter in the constituency. Mr. DeAllie won 15 per cent. There was no split to blame: the DPM and the Grenada Social Democratic Party took 95 votes between them.

One seat also carries outsized national weight. This year St. George South alone supplied 12.4 per cent of every vote the NDC won nationally. A party that writes this seat off is ignoring one in every eight votes its opponent won.
