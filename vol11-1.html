After Bowen: The Second Test in St. George South East

Gregory Bowen, a former Deputy Prime Minister, lost St. George South East in 2022. In 2026 the NNP tried a new candidate, and the result was worse: a margin of 1,294, no polling divisions, and a fall in the NNP's share faster than the national swing.

For a generation, St. George South East meant Gregory Bowen. He represented the seat for the NNP over many years, served as Deputy Prime Minister and later as the party's deputy chairman. In 2022 he lost it to the NDC's Phillip Telesford by 2,122 votes to 1,634.

"A known name softened the fall. A new one did not."Greg Hosten

The 2026 election was the NNP's second test in the seat, and its first without Mr. Bowen on the ballot. Mr. Telesford took 2,464 votes to 1,170 for the NNP's Dwight Horsford, with 66 for the DPM's Stanford Simon and 10 for the GULP. A deficit of 488 became 1,294.

A seat that has swung before

St. George South East, which runs from Springs and Calivigny through La Borie, Morne Jaloux, St. Paul's and Hope Vale, is not a natural stronghold for either party. Mr. Bowen lost it once before, in 2008, to the NDC's Karl Hood, and won it back in 2013.

That history matters. A seat that has swung both ways can swing back. But it swings towards the party that is present, and in 2026 the numbers suggest the NNP was not.

Worse than the national swing

The NNP's vote fell by 464, from 1,634 to 1,170. Mr. Telesford's rose by 342. The NNP's share of the two-party vote dropped from 43.5 per cent to 32.2 per cent, a fall of 11.3 points, one point worse than the national swing.

In seats where the NNP's candidate was a familiar name, such as St. John and St. Andrew North East, the party fell by less than the national average. In St. George South East, with a new candidate, it fell by more. The pattern is consistent with the rest of this series: a known name softened the fall, and a new one did not.

The incumbent who consolidated

Mr. Telesford entered the 2026 campaign after four years as the sitting member. His share of the whole electorate rose from 39.2 per cent of registered voters to 42.1 per cent, one of the clearest signs in any seat of an incumbent strengthening his position.

The NNP's share of the electorate fell from 30.2 per cent to 20.0 per cent. In four years the party lost a third of its voters in a seat it had held for most of the previous two decades.
